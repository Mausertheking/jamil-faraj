To the admissions committee

Why China

Dear members of the committee,

I have looked toward China since my first years at school. Its rise impressed me, but what stayed with me was the attitude to learning behind it. Two phrases say it better than I can: 尊师重道, to honour teachers and respect the way of knowledge, and 严谨治学, to study with rigour. I want an education that takes mathematics, logic and engineering that seriously, and that expects humility and persistence from its students.

China is also a place where a very old civilisation and very new technology stand side by side. The country that gave the world paper, printing and the compass now works at the front of artificial intelligence, supercomputing and quantum communication. I know a small version of that mix from the piano: seven years of classical training taught me that art, structure and mathematical precision belong together. Computer science is where I want to use it.

I grew up in Baku, an old crossroads of the Silk Road, and Chinese culture and language never felt far away. I don’t want to read about new technology from a distance. I want to live on a Chinese campus, learn from its professors, work in its libraries and laboratories, and learn Chinese properly. One proverb carried me through my piano years: 台上一分钟，台下十年功, one minute on stage takes ten years of work off it. I hope to graduate as a computer scientist who helps connect Azerbaijan and China.

Jamil Faraj
Baku, Azerbaijan
